## 📹 Part 1: Video Response Script
*Note: Below is the verbatim text transcript of my recorded Week 4 video submission.*

"Hello, my name is Alozie Godwin Ogbu-Onya In deeply reflecting on **Doctrine and Covenants 58:26–28**, I have come to realize that as information technology professionals, we are blessed with personal agency—the power to act rather than to be acted upon. In a technical landscape, this means we must proactively design righteous, secure, and accessible digital ecosystems without waiting to be commanded or micromanaged by external compliance mandates. 

Throughout my developmental journey this term, my actions and design decisions have been explicitly anchored by **three guiding principles**:

1. **Data Veracity as Absolute Truth:** Grounded in *John 8:32*, software systems must present clean, unaltered realities. We reject deceptive data patterns or skewed analytics, treating data integrity as a moral duty.
2. **Digital Stewardship and Protection:** Our technical skills are a divine trust. Just as we guard our spiritual lives, we must use encryption, secure access controls, and regular system patching to safeguard the vulnerable from malicious cyber actors.
3. **Empathy through Universal Design:** True Christian charity requires inclusivity. Software systems must be architected so they do not exclude individuals with physical or cognitive challenges, intentionally honoring their inherent worth.

My holistic growth throughout this course has been guided, cross-examined, and enriched by five core structural frameworks and texts: Elder David A. Bednar’s *Gather Together in One All Things in Christ*, Elder Joseph B. Wirthlin’s *Come What May, and Love It*, Elder Jeffrey R. Holland's *Be Not Afraid, Only Believe*, the Association for Computing Machinery (ACM) Code of Ethics, and the World Wide Web Consortium's (W3C) Web Content Accessibility Guidelines. Together, these materials prove that technical excellence is not separate from spiritual discipline—they are gathered together as one."
